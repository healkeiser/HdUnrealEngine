# Unreal post-processing

By default the beauty is linear scene-referred HDR, like Karma's, so colour management and compositing work as usual. UE's post-processing can be applied on top:

Realtime, without and with UE's tonemapper (the linear image is shown with a simple display curve).

<div class="hdue-figure" data-figure="tonemapper"></div>

- `unreal:postProcess:<Property>` sets any [`FPostProcessSettings`](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/Engine/FPostProcessSettings) property by name, and its override flag: `float unreal:postProcess:BloomIntensity = 2`, `float4 unreal:postProcess:ColorSaturation = (0, 0, 0, 1)`. Numbers, booleans, enum names, object paths and tuples (filling a struct's fields in order) are accepted. Unknown names or values are skipped with a warning in the UE log (`HdUEBackend\Saved\Logs`).
- `unreal:postProcessMaterials` lists cooked post-process material paths (a string array, or one string separated by spaces), added as blendables.
- `unreal:tonemapper` runs UE's tonemapper. Bloom, colour grading (saturation, contrast, gamma, gain, offset, white balance, LUTs), the film curve, vignette, grain, chromatic aberration and lens flares only happen inside it, so they need it on. UE's on-screen warnings are never drawn into hdUE frames.

With the tonemapper on (and the tone curve, see [colour management](color-management.md)), the beauty is UE's tone-mapped image as display-linear Rec.709: the film curve's output before UE encodes it for an sRGB monitor, as UE writes float back buffers and Movie Render Queue renders (`SCS_FinalToneCurveHDR`). The config's default view (Houdini's: sRGB display, Un-tone-mapped) then shows what UE shows on an sRGB monitor, in husk's EXR and, apart from the deepest shadows, in the viewport (below); see [colour management](color-management.md) for other configs. Keep that view: Raw shows it too dark. `unreal:disableToneCurve` keeps the tonemapper's grading, bloom, vignette and grain without the film curve: the beauty stays scene-linear for any OCIO view. It does not match Karma, which does not tone map. Grey ramp at exposure 1, beauty value: 0.0625 gives 0.037, 0.18 gives 0.18, 1 gives 0.72, 4 gives 0.94.

**Viewport and husk.** For the same stage and settings, the beauty hdUE gives Houdini's viewport and the one husk writes match: on the Cornell box with the tonemapper on, the viewport's converged frame is 1.03x husk's mean, its 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles within 1.5/255 in sRGB. The offline profile (Cinematic scalability, Lumen's final-frame quality, see [engine quality](engine-quality.md)) is not what separates them either: husk with the viewport's values renders 0.98x. What differs is Houdini's viewport display. With OCIO on (sRGB display, Un-tone-mapped), Houdini 22.0.368's viewport shows linear values below about 0.004 as black and lowers values up to about 0.05, although the same view through OCIO's own CPU processor, and OCIO 2.5's GPU shader for it, are the exact sRGB curve: the short box's front face, (0.0022, 0.0006, 0.0001), is (0, 0, 0) in a flipbook and (7, 2, 0)/255 in husk's EXR through that view. Turning the viewport's OCIO off shows those shadows again (as gamma 2.2). Tone-mapped scenes keep much of the image there (the film curve maps 0.03 to 0.011), so a viewport capture of a dark interior has many black pixels that husk's EXR does not. The EXR is the faithful image; the difference is Houdini's to fix. To reproduce: `Launch-Houdini.ps1 -TestKind parity -InputUsd <stage>` with `HDUE_TEST_CAMERA` set writes the delegate's frame (`viewport.pfm`) and flipbooks with the viewport's OCIO on and off.

UE's film curve keeps middle grey (0.18) and compresses the rest: shadows more than an sRGB view of the linear image does (0.03 becomes 0.011), highlights roll off to white. A scene whose important parts sit far below middle grey, like Sponza's interior at its authored exposure, therefore looks darker with the tonemapper than without, as it would in UE at that exposure; raise `AutoExposureBias` or the camera exposure.

Exposure stays manual unless authored: exposure 1 times 2^`AutoExposureBias` (hdUE's default 0), plus the USD camera exposure in stops, so the tonemapper keeps the linear image's middle grey and frames are deterministic. UE's physical-camera exposure (ISO, shutter, aperture) stays off unless `AutoExposureApplyPhysicalCameraExposure` is set. `AutoExposureMethod` `AEM_Histogram` or `AEM_Basic` turns on UE's auto exposure with UE's defaults (legacy luminance range: `AutoExposureMinBrightness` 0.03, `AutoExposureMaxBrightness` 8); the camera exposure still adds to the bias, so lower it for scenes exposed for Karma. Auto exposure reaches its target in every frame, so husk's warmup frames settle on it; set `AutoExposureSpeedUp` or `AutoExposureSpeedDown` to have the realtime viewport adapt gradually, as in UE (husk and Path Tracer renders always use the target). Auto exposure is not frame-exact: two identical Sponza renders differed by about 7%, and each frame of a sequence is exposed on its own. On Sponza, UE's default auto exposure (Histogram, bias 1) lands about 0.6 stops above the camera's authored exposure.

Depth of field, post-process materials with a "Before Tonemapping" blendable location, and the other settings apply with the tonemapper off too. Post-processing applies to the beauty only; AOVs stay raw. Changing a post-process setting restarts Path Tracer accumulation.

The render settings LOP lists every user-facing `FPostProcessSettings` property (212) in UE's own categories, names, ranges, tooltips and defaults, in the order of UE's Details panel, a tab per UE category ([layout](../houdini/viewport.md)): Lens, Color Grading (with Film and Film Grain), Lighting (Global Illumination, Reflections), Rendering Features, and Path Tracing under Rendering. Lens' effects other than Camera and Depth of Field, Color Grading, Film and Film Grain need the tonemapper and are disabled while it is off. Defaults are UE's, except where hdUE sets its own per view (shown in the tooltips): manual exposure, Lumen GI and reflections from `unreal:lumenEnabled`, auto exposure reaching its target every frame unless a speed is set and depth of field from the USD camera. `scripts/generate_render_settings.py` generates them from the engine source. It leaves out: `WeightedBlendables` (use `unreal:postProcessMaterials`), `PathTracingSamplesPerPixel` (use `unreal:samplesPerPixel`), the mobile depth of field, and the fixed-size arrays `LensFlareTints` and `DepthOfFieldMatteBoxFlags`. Curve properties take a cooked asset path.

Texture properties (Ambient Cubemap, Exposure Metering Mask, convolution bloom Kernel, Dirt Mask Texture, Color Grading LUT, Lens Flare BokehShape, Vignette Texture, Film Grain Texture) take a cooked texture path (`/Game/...`, `/Engine/...`) or an image file (exr, hdr, png, jpg, tif, tga, bmp), which the LOP shows with a file chooser and authors as a USD asset path, so a relative path resolves against its layer: `asset unreal:postProcess:BloomDirtMask = @textures/dirt.png@`. The backend imports a file at render time as UE's texture defaults would: 8-bit files sRGB, float files linear, unfiltered mips; the Color Grading LUT is UE's 256 x 16 strip of 16 slices, read edge to edge (red across a slice, green down, blue across the slices); film grain tiles, the others clamp. Ambient Cubemap takes a lat-long panorama, made a cubemap as UE's texture build makes one (box-filtered mips rather than UE's angular filter); it lights the scene in realtime modes (UE's Path Tracer does not apply it). UE 5.8.4's `AmbientCubemapComposite.usf` never defines `SUBSTRATE_LOAD_FROM_MATERIALCONTAINER`, so with Substrate's adaptive GBuffer (UE's default on SM6, `r.Substrate.ProjectGBufferFormat=1`, and hdUE's) it read the legacy GBuffer, found every pixel unlit and added nothing, in a stock UE 5.8 editor project too ([repro](https://github.com/healkeiser/HdUnrealEngine/blob/main/samples/repro/ambient_cubemap/README.md)); `Apply-ResidencyPatch.ps1` adds the define as UE's other Substrate composites have it ([building](../building.md)). [render_settings/ambient_cubemap](https://github.com/healkeiser/HdUnrealEngine/blob/main/samples/conformance/render_settings/ambient_cubemap.usda) checks that a white cubemap brightens a lit wall. A file is imported again only when its modification time or size changes, so editing the image updates the render and unchanged files cost nothing per frame; a file that cannot be read is skipped with a warning in the UE log. [render_settings/postprocess_texture_file](https://github.com/healkeiser/HdUnrealEngine/blob/main/samples/conformance/render_settings/postprocess_texture_file.usda) masks half the frame with a vignette texture from a PNG.

## Post-process volumes

An `UnrealPostProcessVolume` prim is a UE Post Process Volume (`APostProcessVolume`): a box filling the cube from -1 to 1 of the prim's local space (a Cube prim's default extent), whose post-process settings apply while the camera is inside it or within its blend radius. **hdUE Post Process Volume** (TAB menu > hdUE > Visual Effects, `integrations/houdini/otls/hdue_postprocessvolume.hda`) creates or edits one, as hdUE Local Fog Volume does its prim, with a guide box in Create mode. The prim type is a codeless schema shipped with the delegate (`resources/generatedSchema.usda`, generated from `APostProcessVolume`):

- `unreal:postProcessVolume:<Property>`: UE's Priority, BlendRadius (UE centimetres, around the box), BlendWeight, bEnabled and bUnbound ("Infinite Extent", the whole world), with the actor's defaults as fallbacks (priority 0, 100 cm, weight 1, enabled, bounded).
- `unreal:postProcess:<Property>` and `unreal:postProcessMaterials`: the volume's settings, with the render settings' names and values. Only authored ones override, as UE's override checkboxes; the LOP's control menus start at Do Nothing. Texture settings take cooked paths (the render settings also import image files).

The backend places each as an actor with a box (its bounds, as the volume's brush in a level) and a `UPostProcessComponent`, and blends them into every view with UE's own `UWorld::AddPostProcessingSettings`: volumes by priority, each weighted by its blend weight and the camera's distance to its box over the blend radius. The render settings' `unreal:postProcess:*` apply after the volumes, as Movie Render Queue blends a camera's settings after the world's volumes (`MoviePipelineUtils.cpp`, `DoPostProcessBlend`): a setting authored on the render settings wins over every volume. [render_settings/post_process_volume](https://github.com/healkeiser/HdUnrealEngine/blob/main/samples/conformance/render_settings/post_process_volume.usda) checks a volume around the camera, one out of its blend radius, an unbound one and two overlapping volumes of different priorities, in both modes. As everywhere, exposure and grading need the tonemapper on.

## Decals

An `UnrealDecal` prim is a UE decal (`UDecalComponent`, as a Decal Actor in a level): a box filling the cube from -1 to 1 of the prim's local space, projecting its material along the prim's local down axis (the stage's up axis, negated) onto the surfaces inside it. Its properties are UE's: `unreal:decal:DecalColor` (a tint, white by default), `SortOrder` (overlapping decals) and `FadeScreenSize`; the fade timers, which destroy a game's actor, are left out. Its material is the material bound to the prim when the host maps it to a prepared asset (a Substrate Builder decal material, [Decal materials](../substrate-builder.md#decal-materials), applied in Houdini's viewport), else `unreal:decal:DecalMaterial`, a cooked Deferred Decal material path; without either it draws nothing; a non-decal material is skipped with a warning. The bound material's live parameter edits reach the decal's material instance. A binding authored on an ancestor or through a collection is read when the decal prim itself syncs, so a later edit of that binding shows once the decal is edited too. **hdUE Decal** (TAB menu > hdUE > Visual Effects, `hdue_decal.hda`) creates or edits one, with a guide box in Create mode. `samples/decal/decal.usda` and the in-Houdini test `-TestKind decal` check a red decal on a wall, and that hiding the prim removes it.
